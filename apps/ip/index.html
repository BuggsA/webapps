Questions people ask

What is an IP address?

The number the internet uses to send traffic back to you. Every device that talks to a website does so from an address: four numbers like 203.0.113.7 in IPv4, or hexadecimal groups like 2001:db8::7 in IPv6.

What is my public IP address?

The one shown at the top of this page: the address websites see when you visit them. At home it usually belongs to your router and is shared by every device behind it. Your computer has a different, private address on your own network - check whether an address is private.

How accurate is the location?

It is where your network appears to be, not where you are. It is usually right about the country and often right about the region; a mobile carrier can put you a city away, and a VPN a country away.

Why does it show IPv6 instead of IPv4?

Each visit reaches a site over one or the other, and most devices prefer IPv6 when their network has it. Your IPv6 address explains more, and IPv4 vs IPv6 compares the two.

Who is my internet provider?

The network your address belongs to, named by its AS number. On a VPN or at work, that is whoever’s network you are actually going through. See your provider.

Can someone find me from my IP address?

Not your home. An address points to your provider and roughly to an area; only your provider can match it to an account, and normally only under a legal process.

How do I change or hide my IP address?

A VPN or a privacy relay shows sites a different address. Moving to another network - a phone hotspot, a café - changes it too. Restarting a home router sometimes gets a new one from the provider, but many providers keep the same address for months.
