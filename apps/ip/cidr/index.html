What CIDR notation means

A range like 192.168.1.0/24 is an address and a prefix length. The number after the slash says how many of the leading bits are fixed; the rest are free. IPv4 has 32 bits, so a /24 leaves 8 free bits: 28 = 256 addresses. Every step down doubles it.

Common IPv4 prefixes

/32255.255.255.255 — one address
/30255.255.255.252 — 4 addresses, 2 usable
/29255.255.255.248 — 8 addresses, 6 usable
/28255.255.255.240 — 16 addresses, 14 usable
/27255.255.255.224 — 32 addresses, 30 usable
/26255.255.255.192 — 64 addresses, 62 usable
/25255.255.255.128 — 128 addresses, 126 usable
/24255.255.255.0 — 256 addresses, 254 usable
/22255.255.252.0 — 1,024 addresses
/20255.255.240.0 — 4,096 addresses
/16255.255.0.0 — 65,536 addresses
/8255.0.0.0 — 16,777,216 addresses

Why two fewer usable hosts?

In an ordinary IPv4 subnet the first address names the network and the last is its broadcast address, so neither is given to a device. A /31 is the exception: a point-to-point link with both addresses usable (RFC 3021). IPv6 has no broadcast address.

IPv6 prefixes

IPv6 ranges are written the same way. A single network is normally a /64 - 18 quintillion addresses - and a home is often given a /56 or /48, room for 256 or 65,536 of those networks.
